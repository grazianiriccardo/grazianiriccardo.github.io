I study how democratization and electoral reform changed political representation, using two centuries of debate in the Belgian parliament.

My fields are political economy, economic history and urban economics, and I work with text as data. I built a dataset of the plenary speeches of the Belgian Chamber of Representatives since 1831, linked to the members who delivered them, and I use it to measure what parliament attends to and how far apart its parties speak, around franchise extensions, proportional representation and the linguistic split of the party system. A second line of work uses geospatial data from historical maps to study railways and transport networks and their political effects.

Before my PhD, I studied economics at Bocconi University and at UCLouvain (joint MSc), and worked as a research assistant for James M. Snyder and Marco Tabellini at Harvard and for Stefano Gagliarducci at the University of Rome Tor Vergata.
